The history behind the picture

Warwick Brothers & Rutter: a wide view of the Island

With thanks to Harry Holman’s illustrated catalogues at Straitpost, the starting point for these albums, expanded here with cards from the collection.

Warwick Brothers & Rutter was a Toronto printing and publishing firm with a large Canadian postcard business. Its Island cards range from public buildings and busy wharves to the intimate pleasures of a country walk or a day beside a trout stream. Printed photographer credits, including W. S. Louson’s, preserve part of the story behind the pictures. Colour, captions, patriotic borders and souvenir formats could give a single photograph several lives.

The company’s Toronto factory glows with light on postcard 4106. Turn it over and the printed message promises a visit from Geo. Hazen: a small reminder of the sales calls behind the cards that reached Island shops.

Photographs that travelled

The steamer, its trailing smoke and the clouds above it make this sunset easy to follow. Warwick issued the picture twice as Sunset on Lake Ontario, under numbers 1267 and 1296. The same scene appears on 1692 as a ship entering Charlottetown harbour. A new caption carried a familiar photograph across provincial boundaries.

Two anglers beside a wooded bank made a similar journey. They appear on Fishing on Humber River, Toronto, number 2065, and on Trout Fishing, Souris, number 1828, which carries W. S. Louson’s photographic credit. Another printing of 2065 adds gold lettering for the IOOF Sovereign Grand Lodge in Toronto in 1906. The photograph became a souvenir of a gathering as well as a place.

The black-and-white sample set67 entries

These 67 black-and-white cards offer a quieter view of familiar scenes: the road through Bonshaw, Montague’s waterfront, children beneath blossoming trees. Their backs have no printed address panel, stamp box or publisher’s imprint; many carry handwritten identifications. They may have served as a salesman’s sample set. Their shared numbers make it easy to compare this spare presentation with the fuller captions and colour of the familiar editions.

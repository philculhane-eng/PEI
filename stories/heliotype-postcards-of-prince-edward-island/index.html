The history behind the picture

Heliotype: the Island collection

A fisherman stands beside the Montague River. Cars line up in Charlottetown. At Port Borden, railway tracks run straight towards the ferry. These quiet, mostly sepia views belong to a printing family that can be easy to miss when cards are filed under the names of their publishers.

This album grew from Phil Culhane’s contribution to Mike Smith’s forthcoming book on Heliotype Postcard Co. cards. It brings the collection’s Heliotype findings together, with the backs that identify the two main families shown before their pictures.

In this album: 16 confirmed Heliotype cards in two back families, plus a PECO comparison. The cards include work published for Valentine & Sons, Carter & Co. and Pugh. Repeated copies are linked beneath a representative front.

The HTC monogram · The plain heading and shield · The PECO comparison

The HTC monogram

Identifying back · ornamental heading and wavy stamp box

Shaded letters spell out “Post Card” above a double rule. The stamp box holds the interlocked HTC monogram, with “Design Registd” underneath. This is the design shown as HEL-back 1D in Smith’s supplied guide. The publisher’s name can change along the left edge while the printer’s design remains recognisable.

The two Port Borden terminal cards carry the same title but show different views. Both are included here.

The plain heading and shield

Identifying back · serif heading, T-shaped divider and shield

The second family uses a simpler serif heading and a small “Made in Canada” shield inside a rectangular stamp box. Its layout corresponds to HEL-back 2C in Smith’s guide. The complete layout matters: similar shields also occur on PECO cards.

W. S. Louson’s name appears on the ferry views at Cape Tormentine and Port Borden. The printer, publisher and photographer each played a part in bringing the picture to a postcard rack.

A PECO comparison: S.S. Empress

The printed company name settles this comparison

This Digby, Nova Scotia, card was included in the early Heliotype research list. Its back explicitly credits the Photogelatine Engraving Co., and it is shown here as a PECO comparison. The shield alone cannot identify the printer. The card also appears in the PECO album.

About the research

The starting sources were Smith’s supplied Heliotype back-type guide, draft P.E.I. handbook pages and the collector’s correspondence with Smith. The three named issues in those draft pages—Shaw’s Hotel, Trout Fishing at Montague and the Prince Edward Island car ferry—are represented here by 0680, 3848 and 0580. The wider collection review supplied the other views. Smith also confirmed River View (4218), Victoria Hotel (4281) and Hampton Beach (4437), as reported by Culhane in November 2026.

The album records the findings currently supported by that project. Additional issues and back variants may yet come to light. Published-for credits remain visible on the individual cards; Heliotype is identified here as the printer.

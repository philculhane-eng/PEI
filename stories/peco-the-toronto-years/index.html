The history behind the picture

PECO’s Island Album: The Toronto Years

A blue sky, a white border, and somewhere on the Island worth sending home. PECO’s late postcards make a wonderfully generous album: town streets, church spires, ferry crossings, quiet rivers and a great deal of summer.

This gathering began with the back of one Montague postcard, 2314. Turn it over and there is a recognisable arrangement: POST CARD above CARTE POSTALE, the company’s Toronto imprint running down the middle, and a little PECO maple-leaf emblem at its foot. Follow that design through the collection and an Island tour unfolds.

A holiday, one card at a time

The pleasures accumulate as you move through the pictures. At Montague, the buildings and bridge settle into their reflections. At Dalvay, people throw their arms into the surf. The Summerside tourist bureau waits beside the road, while a row of waterfront cabins offers another place to unpack. There are golf links, beaches, a hotel entrance and a winter street in Kensington. A postcard rack could offer several seasons at once.

Farming sits comfortably beside holiday-making. Potato fields, a harvest scene, cattle in shallow water and the patchwork of the “Million Acre Farm” belong to the same album as the swimmers. The captions encourage the visitor to admire a working countryside. Some are wonderfully expansive: Montrose’s trout pond is introduced as a fishing attraction and a source of electric power.

The Abegweit receives several views, including a glimpse inside at the companionway leading to the lounge. The crossing itself was something to put on a postcard. Alongside the ship pictures, a map of routes to the Maritime national parks turns the collection into a small invitation to travel.

Then there are the quiet black-and-white IBR cards: churches in Charlottetown and Belfast, the water at Murray River, a shoreline at Lower Montague. Their different appearance makes the shared back particularly useful. Together, the cards let us move between the colourful souvenir Island and the smaller details of its buildings and communities.

The Toronto clue

PECO was the Photogelatine Engraving Company. It was incorporated in Ottawa in 1920 and moved its main operation to Toronto in 1947. Ken L. Elder’s history for the Toronto Postcard Club places its Toronto years between 1947 and the company’s closure in 1954. He also records that much of its output in those years reused earlier colour, black-and-white and sepia designs. The Toronto imprint therefore helps date the edition; the photograph may reach further back. Elder’s PECO history

A contemporary government directory supplies a firm address: the Printing Trades report for 1949 lists Photogelatine Engraving Co. Ltd. at 3551 Danforth Avenue, Toronto. These small Island souvenirs belonged to a printing business serving a much wider market. The 1949 directory, page M-29

A number can contain a surprise

PECO no. 1 gives us two Queen Street views: one at street level, another from above. No. 37 takes us to two different Cavendish Beach scenes. No. 10 appears on different race-track pictures. Reading the number gets us started; looking at the image finishes the job.

The Charlottetown Hotel adds a particularly enjoyable variation. One version has a station wagon in the foreground, while another has a sedan. Both appear here, so the difference remains visible. The school at Summerside and the views of Rochford Square offer more reasons to linger over pictures with familiar captions.

Several fronts carry an “N.F.B. Photo” credit, including the aerial view, Grafton Street and the “Million Acre Farm.” Those printed credits are useful leads for the larger PECO story. The company’s common back brings the editions together, while individual photographs can have histories of their own.

For now, this is a chance to spread the cards out and enjoy the Island they assemble. A little Montague reflection has led to an album large enough for a very satisfying journey.

About the grouping and sources

Membership follows the back design of 2314: the two-line bilingual heading, horizontal rule, Toronto company imprint and PECO Canada leaf emblem. Blue and black printing belong to this family. The collection also contains earlier Ottawa backs and other Toronto layouts, which form neighbouring chapters in PECO’s history.

The reverse scans of the catalogued PECO cards were reviewed, with an image-matching search across the available collection backs to find examples filed under other publishers or left unattributed. Accepted candidates were checked against the scans. The totals describe this collection’s present holdings. The gallery separates different views and materially changed picture versions, while linking repeated copies and smaller print variations. Short display titles follow the printed captions, with spelling and punctuation regularised.

The printed front and back attached to record 1499 identify PECO no. 52; its current catalogue description names Valentine’s. It is linked here as another copy of the Green Gables view. The representative is 1623. Record 2592 also joins the group through its printed PECO back, although its publisher field is blank.

Company dates and the history of reprinting come from Ken L. Elder’s study linked above; the Danforth Avenue address is independently recorded in the 1949 government report. Scene descriptions and grouping comparisons come from the postcards. Prefixes such as IBR and LN are retained as printed.

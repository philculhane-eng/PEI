PECO’s Toronto Years: The Numbered Album

A blue sky, a white border, and somewhere on the Island worth sending home. PECO’s late numbered postcards make a generous tour: town streets, church spires, ferry crossings, quiet rivers and a great deal of summer.

This gathering began with the back of a Montague postcard, 2314. Its bilingual heading, Toronto imprint and little PECO maple leaf led to a much larger family. That family now has three albums. This page follows the ordinary PECO number sequence; the IBR cards and the other issues have their own pages, linked below.

A holiday, one number at a time

The pleasures accumulate as you move through the pictures. At Montague, the buildings and bridge settle into their reflections. The Summerside tourist bureau waits beside the road. There are golf links, beaches, a hotel entrance and a long succession of places where a visitor might pause. A postcard rack could offer a very full Island holiday.

Farming sits comfortably beside holiday-making. Potato fields, a harvest scene, the “Million Acre Farm” and the patchwork of rural views belong to the same album as the swimmers. The captions invite admiration for a working countryside. Montrose’s trout pond is introduced as a fishing attraction and a source of electric power.

The Abegweit has its places in the number sequence too. A ferry crossing could be a postcard subject in its own right: the journey to the Island was already part of the holiday.

A number can contain a surprise

There are 57 different PECO numbers represented in these 64 entries. No. 1 gives us two Queen Street views: one at street level, another from above. No. 37 takes us to two Cavendish Beach scenes. No. 10 appears on different race-track pictures. Those distinct pictures remain side by side under their shared numbers.

The Charlottetown Hotel adds an enjoyable variation. One version has a station wagon in the foreground, while another has a sedan. Both appear here. The school at Summerside, Rochford Square and Green Gables offer more reasons to linger over familiar numbers and captions.

Several fronts carry an “N.F.B. Photo” credit, including the aerial view, Grafton Street and the “Million Acre Farm.” Those printed credits are useful leads for the larger PECO story. The common back brings these editions together, while individual photographs can have histories of their own.

The Toronto clue

PECO was the Photogelatine Engraving Company. Ken L. Elder’s history for the Toronto Postcard Club dates the move of its main operation from Ottawa to Toronto to 1947 and its closure to 1954. Earlier colour, black-and-white and sepia designs continued to be used during those years. The Toronto imprint therefore helps date an edition; its photograph may reach further back. Elder’s PECO history

The government’s Printing Trades report for 1949 lists Photogelatine Engraving Co. Ltd. at 3551 Danforth Avenue, Toronto. These Island souvenirs belonged to a printing business serving a much wider market. The 1949 directory, page M-29

About this album

These cards belong to the back-design family of postcard 2314: the two-line bilingual heading, horizontal rule, Toronto company imprint and PECO maple-leaf emblem. Blue and black printing belong to this family. The collection’s earlier Ottawa backs and other Toronto layouts have their own guide.

The former 98-entry Toronto album is now divided between three pages: 23 IBR entries, 64 ordinary numbered PECO entries and 11 other issues. Together they retain all 158 cards from that album. Different pictures under one number remain separate; repeated photographs and small printing variations share an entry. Display titles regularise spelling and punctuation.

Record 1499’s attached scans identify PECO 52, although its catalogue description names Valentine’s; it remains linked as another copy of the Green Gables view represented by 1623. Record 2592 joins through its printed PECO back, while its publisher field is blank. Company dates and reprinting history follow the sources linked above.

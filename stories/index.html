Stories from the collection.

  • PEI’s Ten Greatest Postcards

    Nine cards I can put on the table, and room for a tenth. Each image opens the story and its companion postcards.

  • Yours truly, Alice Maude Kinch

    A signed portrait, two Island family histories and an iceboat survivor’s own account take us from Cascumpec to a dentist’s family in Massachusetts.

  • Carter’s Island in Amber

    Red captions, apricot skies and four different pictures of Rosebank under one name. An alphabetical gallery of 22 Carter views and one unsigned match.

  • “Here Is a Pretty View for You”: Dalvay in 1901

    Effie sent a picture of a private summer house and news of market day to a friend in Massachusetts. Her oversized postcard is the earliest Island-postmarked view of P.E.I. in the collection.

  • Five Cents at Wonderland

    A sign over a Market Square doorway leads to a five-cent picture show in Charlottetown in 1907. Two newspaper clippings tell us what audiences saw and heard. Three postcard senders tell us what took them to the square.

  • PEI’s Greatest Postcards – #4 – Frank Snake in Two Summerside Postcards

    A pencilled name, two editions of one portrait, and three destinations. What the Summerside publishers printed and what the senders added.

  • From Board Ice to Board Ice

    Crossing the Strait by iceboat. Eleven postcard views, a two-dollar ticket and the memories of a passenger who wore the wrong shoes for the journey.

  • From Government House to G3MY: The QSL Cards of Prince Edward Island

    In 1958, Walter Hyndman crossed out his home address on a radio card and wrote “Government House.” Seven illustrated chapters follow the Island’s shortwave operators and distant listeners from 1927 to 1959.

  • PEI’s Greatest Postcards – #5 – A Christmas Card from One Chief to Another

    Chief Matthew Francis signed a Christmas greeting to a chief in Truro. A second Pictou portrait helps put names to people in the first.

  • From St. James to Senator Yeo’s Gate

    A red monument at Port Hill leads to three houses in the 1880 atlas, a shipbuilding family, and two men at a gate whom the photographer left unnamed.

  • J. A. S. Bayer, Charlottetown Photographer

    A Halifax photographer takes over an Island studio. His shop-window displays, fox trains, college portraits and a day at Stanhope trace a career from 1901 to 1928.

  • PEI’s Greatest Postcards – #8 – Largest Ever Caught on P.E. Island

    A 1911 message dates Pugh’s famous lobster picture. A factory view and a pair from Mount Stewart show what canning meant along the Island shore.

  • Two Lights in Line: A Postcard Map of Prince Edward Island

    Follow 58 lighthouse photographs around Prince Edward Island and across to Cape Tormentine. A matching Kodak back and one addressed card help tell the story.

  • Looking for “Louson Photo”

    Two ways of signing an Island photograph. Browse the unnumbered views by caption and twelve “Louson Photo” views by the numbers on the prints.

  • Two Fairmiles and a Warehouse on the Montague Waterfront

    Two hull numbers lead to wartime rescues and two Montague owners. A warehouse sign and an earlier sender’s note recover the trade along the wharf.

  • The Montague Furnishing Company’s Casket Cards

    A labelled view of the Montague factory, a furniture order from 1900, and seven casket designs traced through the company’s advertising postcards.

  • PEI’s Greatest Postcards – #2 – The New Railway Station in Montague

    A ten-year wait for Montague’s first-train postcard, a morning timetable, Georgetown’s station sample and the steamer contract that served the same waterfront.

  • PEI’s Greatest Postcards – #10 – My Heart’s Desire

    The distance between my bid and the winning one was sixty-five cents. I’ve left a postcard-sized space for the copy I still hope to find.

  • A Little Island News for Mrs. Whidden

    An Alberton souvenir carried news of a burial, an ailing aunt and an offer of work to Shubenacadie. A family stone at North Bedeque may tell us who Mrs. Hooper was.

  • The Railway Stations of Prince Edward Island

    Choose a town on the October 1919 railway map. Eleven station albums take you from the locomotive at Tignish to Montague’s first train and the platform at Murray Harbour.

  • “Some of Our Near Neighbors” — Mi’kmaw Lives on Island Postcards

    A Christmas greeting from Richmond, Sally Mitchell’s name in an archival record, and a basket taking shape. Mi’kmaw lives come into view on both sides of these Island postcards.

  • “You Can Find Me Here Too”: The S.S. Anna at Montague

    Two cats, fourteen men and a note signed “R.” A Norwegian crew posed at Montague in 1913 while Island produce was being loaded for Havana.

  • PEI’s Greatest Postcards – #7 – The Night St. Dunstan’s Burned

    Four timed photographs, Walter Stiff’s Richmond Street imprint and a Cape Cod family trail. Pre-fire and rebuilt interiors show what the congregation lost and recovered.

  • PEI’s Greatest Postcards – #9 – The Card That Started My Collection

    A photograph of four men in a carriage led me to a newspaper portrait of W. Stetson Rogers and the story of the Odd Fellows gathering in Charlottetown in August 1908.

  • Looking West: The E.J.L. Postcards of Southeastern P.E.I.

    A rowboat at Murray Harbour and sails at Belle River lead to five consecutive E.J.L. designs, photographer Elliot Lumsden, and eight more Island views to compare.

  • The Island in the Post, 1877–1949

    Four charts and a map-reading puzzle from the postcard collection. Follow the surviving postmarks by year, month and town, from a bank cashier’s penny card to a summer at Dalvay.

  • PEI’s Greatest Postcards – #3 – The Million-Dollar Fox Train

    Bayer’s train portrait names three fox-industry men. A baggage master, a director’s signature and photographs from four Island ranches help explain the value of live breeding stock.

  • The Strait by Ferry

    Trace the two-ferry round trip on the 1941 ticket, then explore seven Strait crossings and fourteen ship albums, from Stanley and Minto to the original Abegweit.

  • PEI’s Greatest Postcards – #1 – The Tunnel

    A train hidden on the address side, an iceboat crossing between the same capes, and three senders with their own reasons to want a tunnel.

  • The View from the Minister’s Window

    Five Cardigan cards identify Edwin Smith’s waterfront photographs. A captain’s greeting, a Woodville message and a parishioner’s Sunday letter give them a life beyond the printer.

  • PEI’s Greatest Postcards – #6 – Troops Embarking at Charlottetown

    Island artillerymen and two gun barrels aboard the Cacouna. A second photograph, the ship’s reported load, and an August 3 family message place the departure in the first days of war.

  • “Hope We Have Got Rid of the Hoodoo”

    Engine trouble brought the Village Belle to Souris in August 1914. Six lines to Susan Knight, two newspaper clippings and a constable’s notebook follow an Arctic expedition from an Island wharf to Baker Lake and beyond.

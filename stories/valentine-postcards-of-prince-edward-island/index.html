The history behind the picture

Valentine postcards of Prince Edward Island

Valentine’s Island postcards were issued and reissued with changing company names, backs, borders and captions. These albums bring together the known P.E.I. views and related ferries, using cards from this collection wherever possible and credited reference illustrations for other confirmed issues.

The back and printing define each album. A repeated photograph or number does not make two editions the same card. This is a working catalogue: the published list has gaps and conflicting numbers, and the entries below distinguish examined cards from views still known only through that list.

Other publisher imprints: Valentine–Carter editions have their own albums on the Carter page. McCoy overprints and Maddigan’s Valentine-numbered cards are likewise shown with those publishers, rather than repeated here. The Canada Railway News imprints can be compared on postcards 0724, 0725, 0780, 1370, 1661, 2883, 2723. The James MacLean & Sons Northumberland card, 111,348, is another publisher’s edition of a Valentine-numbered photograph. Harry Holman also records an Archambault overprint of Near Souris, 100,925; other Archambault Island views are not established.

Sources and the catalogue

Toronto Postcard Club’s publisher checklists, especially the May 2024 Valentine database, provide the numbered starting point. The source rows were checked against both sides of the collection cards and additional examples. The list records photographs and observations, not a complete inventory of every physical edition.

Harry Holman’s railway-card study, double-postcard article and McCoy and Archambault study help identify formats and secondary imprints. Additional illustrations are credited individually to UPEI’s digital collections.

Holman reports at least nine monochrome McCoy overprints. The linked McCoy page shows the individually identified examples; the full reported set still needs an item-by-item source.

The number 115,871 in the list conflicts with the clearly printed 118,871 on the Green Gables footbridge cards. Those examined editions are shown under 118,871; the unconfirmed alternative has not been counted as another photograph. Faint numerals and unseen backs remain open questions.

Further Valentine cards held for mainland subjects or Island postal use can be seen individually: 2132, 2247, 2333, 2362, 3160, 3191, 4436. They do not establish additional P.E.I. views.

PECO’s IBR Album

Belfast Church begins the journey. Then the numbers carry us into Charlottetown, past church doors and spires, and out again towards rivers, farms and the shore.

The IBR cards form a distinct numbered group within PECO’s late Toronto output. They share the bilingual back that first brought this larger gathering together, while their IBR prefix gives them an album of their own. Here the printed numbers lead the way.

IBR number order

Following the IBR numbers

The opening numbers give the album a strong architectural character. IBR 1 shows Belfast Church. IBR 2 takes us to St. James in Charlottetown, followed by St. Dunstan’s, Trinity United, Zion, the Salvation Army, Central Christian, Holy Redeemer, the Baptist church and St. Peter’s. Seen together, they make a small tour of buildings around which congregations gathered.

Further along, the captions carry us to Murray Harbour, High Bank, Cardigan and Murray River. A harvest scene appears at IBR 19, followed by Lower Montague and Victoria Park. The sequence brings public buildings, quiet water and the working countryside into the same pocket-sized tour.

Dalvay has three places here: the beach at IBR 22, the house at IBR 27 and surf bathing at IBR 28. New Glasgow, the Confederation Chamber and a national park beach broaden the excursion. The numbers let us bring scattered copies together and see those neighbouring subjects side by side.

The present holdings contain 23 numbered views, with additional copies of Belfast Church, St. James and the Confederation Chamber linked beneath their pictures. The sequence has gaps; its original extent remains a subject for further collecting.

About this album

These cards belong to the back-design family of postcard 2314: the two-line bilingual heading, horizontal rule, Toronto company imprint and PECO maple-leaf emblem. Blue and black printing belong to this family. The collection’s earlier Ottawa backs and other Toronto layouts have their own guide.

The former 98-entry Toronto album is now divided between three pages: 23 IBR entries, 64 ordinary numbered PECO entries and 11 other issues. Together they retain all 158 cards from that album. Different pictures under one number remain separate; repeated photographs and small printing variations share an entry. Display titles regularise spelling and punctuation.

The IBR prefix is retained exactly as printed. The grouping establishes a numbered issue; the initials’ expansion and the photographs’ authorship remain open research questions.

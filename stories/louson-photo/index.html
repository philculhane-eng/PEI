The history behind the picture

Looking for “Louson Photo”

Look near the bottom of the wharf on this postcard. A man in a cap stands with his back to us, facing the Plant Line steamer Halifax. Down by his feet the photographer has left three words in white, “Photo by Louson.” On a card of men fishing the Dunk River, the credit takes a different form. A pale strip below the photograph gives the title, the words “Louson Photo,” and a tiny number 10.

William Steele Louson came from Montreal to Charlottetown in 1900. He earned his living as a wholesale sales agent, work that took him around the Island. His photographs found readers in the Prince Edward Island Magazine and the Montreal Standard, and publishers put his views on picture postcards. Some of his pictures survive chiefly in that form. The scarcer signed real-photo cards in my collection offer a way to look at his work a few inches at a time. Harry Holman’s Louson exhibition at the Confederation Centre

In the Dunk River picture, two figures stand on the bank and a third is down at the boat. The eye has to find its way between the white birches to see them. Number 19 in the same caption style could hardly be more different. The icebreaker Earl Grey fills the width of the card, smoke above the funnel and small dark figures down on the ice beside her bow. There was room for a working ship as well as a trout stream in Louson’s Island. The icebreaker and her number, 4610

Number 12 is a vertical view in Victoria Park. At first the two pale figures across the water are easier to see as reflections. On the reverse, a family wrote from Prince Edward Island on August 8 about seed potatoes and silver-fox farms. The stamp went through Quebec the next afternoon, August 9, 1927. Louson had died in 1921. Here is a photograph that went on introducing visitors to the Island years after the man who made it was gone. The pictures and their little numbers leave a question for a longer story. How many views in that sequence can we put back together? The Victoria Park print and its dated back, 3727
